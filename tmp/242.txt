The cmps instructions, summarized in Fig. 7.7, compare elements of source and
destination strings. Chapter 5 explained how a cmp instruction subtracts two operands
and sets flags based on the difference. Similarly, cmps subtracts two string elements and
sets flags based on the difference; neither operand is changed. If a cmps instruction is
used in a loop, it is appropriate to follow cmps by almost any of the conditional jump
instructions, depending on the design being implemented.
Repeat prefixes are often used with cmps instructions. In fact, for the task of
finding if two strings are identical, the repe prefix is a perfect companion for cmps. Figure 7.7 summarizes all the cmps instructions, including repeated ones. Again, the timings are not strictly accurate for 486 and Pentium CPUs; for rep cmps there are special
timings when n=0.
