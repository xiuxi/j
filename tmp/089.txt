The number of bytes taken by a memory operand depends on the type of
operand. A direct operand must be encoded as a 32-bit address, four bytes. A register indirect operand is encoded as three bits in the second object code byte. We will later examine
encodings of other types of memory operands. The 66 prefix byte is again required for 16bit operands; it is not shown in the table since it is technically not part of the instruction.
The C6 and C7 opcodes listed in Fig. 4.2 for immediate-to-memory moves can
also be used for immediate-to-register moves. However, these forms require an extra
byte of object code, and an assembler normally chooses the shorter form given in Fig. 4.1.

EBX, ECX, EDX, EBP, ESI, EDI, or ESP. Similarly register 16 refers to one of the 16 bit
registers AX, BX, CX, DX, SP, BP, SI or DI, and register 8 refers to an eight bit register,
AL, AH, BL, BH, CL, CH, DL, or DH.
