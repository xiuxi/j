398 Decimal Arithmetic
The das instruction (decimal adjust after subtraction) is used after a sub or sbb
instruction. It acts like the daa except that 6 or 6016 is subtracted from rather than added
to the value in AL. The following examples show how das works following sub al,bl.
In the first example, both CF and AF are set to 1 since the subtraction requires borrows
in both digit positions. When 6 and 6016 are subtracted from BC, the result is 56, and
both CF and AF remain set to 1. This is the correct answer since 25 – 69 = 56 (borrowing
1 to change 25 into 125.)

Each of the daa and das instructions encodes in a single byte. The daa instruction has opcode 27 and the das instruction has opcode 2F. Each requires three clock
cycles to execute on a Pentium. In addition to modifying AF and CF, the SF, ZF and PF
flags are set or reset by daa or das instructions to correspond to the final value in AL.
The overflow flag OF is undefined and other flags are not affected.
